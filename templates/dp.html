Background Info

Differential privacy is a system for publicly sharing information about a dataset by describing the patterns of groups within the dataset while withholding information about individuals in the dataset. It is a strong form of privacy protection that has been widely studied in the academic community and is increasingly being adopted in practice. Differential privacy provides a mathematically rigorous definition of privacy that has proven to be useful in a wide variety of contexts.

Although differential privacy has a ton of mathmatical work behind it, DO NOT WORRY! We will take care of all the math for you. We will break down the concept of differential privacy and show you how to apply it to a dataset in a simple and intuitive way.

High Level

At a high level, differential privacy is a property of a data analysis or transformation algorithm that ensures that the output of the algorithm does not reveal sensitive information about individual data points. In other words, differential privacy guarantees that the output of the algorithm is essentially the same whether a particular individual's data is included or excluded from the input dataset. This property is achieved by adding carefully calibrated noise to the computation in a way that preserves the overall patterns in the data while protecting the privacy of individuals.

Differential privacy is a powerful tool for protecting privacy in a wide variety of settings, from social media to healthcare to government data. It is increasingly being used by companies and organizations to protect the privacy of their users and customers while still being able to analyze and share data.

How strong is DP?

Differential privacy is a strong form of privacy protection because it provides a mathematical guarantee that an individual's data will not be affected by their presence in the dataset. This guarantee holds even if the adversary has auxiliary information about the individual. This makes differential privacy particularly useful in settings where the adversary may have access to additional information about the individuals in the dataset.

Key words to know

Query:

A query is a function that takes a dataset as input and produces a result. For example, a query might calculate the sum of a column in a dataset, or count the number of rows that satisfy a certain condition. Queries are used to extract information from a dataset.

Epsilon:

Epsilon is a parameter that controls the amount of privacy protection provided by a differentially private algorithm. A smaller value of epsilon provides stronger privacy protection, but may result in less accurate output. Epsilon is a key parameter in differential privacy that allows users to trade off privacy and accuracy.

Sensitivity:

Sensitivity is a measure of how much the output of a query changes when a single individual's data is added or removed from the dataset. Queries with higher sensitivity are more likely to leak information about individuals in the dataset. Sensitivity is an important concept in differential privacy because it helps to quantify the privacy risk associated with a particular query.

Noise:

Noise is random data that is added to the output of a differentially private algorithm to protect the privacy of individuals in the dataset. The noise is carefully calibrated to ensure that the output of the algorithm is accurate while still providing strong privacy guarantees. Noise is a key component of differential privacy that helps to protect individuals' privacy.
