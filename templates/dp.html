Background Info

Differential privacy is a system for publicly sharing information about a dataset by describing the patterns of groups within the dataset while withholding information about individuals in the dataset. Differential privacy is a strong form of privacy protection that has been widely studied in the academic community and is increasingly being adopted in practice. Differential privacy provides a mathematically rigorous definition of privacy that has proven to be useful in a wide variety of contexts.

Differential privacy is a property of a data analysis or transformation algorithm. An algorithm is differentially private if the outcome of the analysis or transformation is (nearly) the same whether an individual's data is included or excluded from the input dataset. This means that an adversary who knows the output of the analysis cannot determine whether a particular individual's information is included in the input dataset. Differential privacy is achieved by introducing a carefully calibrated amount of noise into the computation. This noise helps protect individuals' privacy while still allowing the algorithm to provide useful output.

Differential privacy is a strong form of privacy protection because it provides a mathematical guarantee that an individual's data will not be affected by their presence in the dataset. This guarantee holds even if the adversary has auxiliary information about the individual. This makes differential privacy particularly useful in settings where the adversary may have access to additional information about the individuals in the dataset.
