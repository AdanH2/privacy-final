K-Anonymity

What is k-anonymity?

The k-anonymity technique makes it so that each record in a dataset cannot be told apart from at least k-1 separate records. K-anonymity makes it difficult for attackers to identify individual records by generalizing and suppressing data. Suppression and generalizations are done on quasi-identifiers to protect sensitive attributes from being connected to specific records. Two other statistics that we visualize are l-diversity and t-closeness. l-diversity assures that for each unique group of quasi-identifiers, there are at least l unique sensitive attributes. t-closeness is the similarity between the distributions of SAs in anonymized data compared to the whole dataset.

Key words to know

Quasi-identifiers: Attributes that can be used to identify an individual. These are the attributes that are generalized and suppressed to protect the sensitive attributes.
Sensitive attributes: Attributes that are sensitive and need to be protected. These are the attributes that are not generalized or suppressed.
k-anonymity: A dataset is k-anonymous if each record in the dataset cannot be distinguished from at least k-1 other records.
l-diversity: A dataset is l-diverse if for each unique group of quasi-identifiers, there are at least l unique sensitive attributes.
t-closeness: A dataset is t-close if the distributions of sensitive attributes in the dataset are similar to the distributions of the sensitive attributes in the whole dataset.
